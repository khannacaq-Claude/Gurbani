Fabric Glossary: What's the Difference, Really?

Fabric names get thrown around a lot, and several of them sound close enough to blur together. Here's what actually separates them — texture, weight, drape, and where each one earns its place.

Georgette

Lightweight, slightly crinkled surface, good flow

A sheer, lightweight fabric with a subtle crêpe-like texture — it has a bit more body and grip than chiffon, so it holds embroidery and embellishment a little more confidently while still flowing well in a lehenga or dupatta.

Chiffon

Sheer, smooth, almost weightless

Smoother and more slippery than georgette, with a finer, sheerer weave. It drapes beautifully and moves with the body, but it's more delicate and less able to carry heavy embellishment on its own — usually lined or paired with a heavier under-layer.

Georgette vs chiffon, in short: georgette has more texture and grip; chiffon is smoother and sheerer.

Crepe

Slightly crinkled, matte finish, good structure

Has a distinctive fine, crinkled texture and a matte (non-shiny) finish. It holds its shape well, doesn't crease easily, and drapes with a bit more structure than chiffon or georgette — a common choice for gowns and fitted silhouettes.

Cotton Silk

A cotton-silk blend, breathable with a soft sheen

Blends cotton's breathability with silk's soft sheen and drape — cooler and easier to wear for long hours than pure silk, while still looking a step above plain cotton. A popular choice for daytime festive wear and pooja functions.

Raw Silk

Textured, matte, less shiny than pure silk

Has a coarser, more textured weave than pure/mulberry silk, with visible slubs and a matte rather than glossy finish. It's sturdier and holds structure well — often used for jackets, blouses, and winter-appropriate pieces.

Raw silk vs pure silk: raw silk is textured and matte; pure silk is smoother and glossier.

Organza

Sheer but crisp — holds its shape, doesn't cling

Also sheer like chiffon, but stiffer and crisper — it stands away from the body slightly rather than clinging, which gives skirts and dupattas volume and structure. A common choice for layered or voluminous silhouettes.

Net

Open mesh weave, sheer, used for overlays

An open, mesh-like weave — sheerer and more delicate than organza, typically used as an overlay, dupatta, or base for heavy embellishment (sequins, stones) rather than as a garment's only layer.

Net vs organza: net is an open mesh, usually layered over something; organza is a solid (if sheer) weave that can stand on its own.

Brocade

Richly woven with raised, often metallic patterns

A heavier fabric woven with a raised, often metallic pattern built directly into the weave (rather than embroidered on afterward) — commonly used for blouses, jackets, and winter festive wear where structure and richness matter more than lightness.

Chanderi

Light, sheer, with a subtle natural sheen

A traditional handwoven fabric (cotton, silk, or a blend) known for its lightweight feel and a soft, glowing sheen — comfortable enough for long wear, which is part of why it works well for pooja and daytime festive occasions.

Satin

Glossy on the front, matte on the back, catches light

Defined by its weave (not its fiber) — a tight weave that gives one glossy, reflective side and one matte side. It catches indoor and evening lighting well, which is why it shows up often in party and reception wear.
